I designed all three surfaces, tested two rounds with the people who handle recalls, and replayed real history to check it.

Problem

Recalls are found late, and drivers find out by mail.

I was working at Sunrise Chevy when the 2021 Bolt battery recall hit. The warning signs had been sitting in owner complaints for months. In 2025, James Rampton’s workshop on realistic mockups for automotive UX gave me the method to design for the car itself.

Found late

A recall usually starts after harm piles up. Every car in my data had warning reports before its first fire.

Told by mail

Owners still learn about a safety recall from a first-class letter.

Fixed slowly

About 70% of recalled cars are repaired within 18 months, so the driver side matters as much as detection.

Repair rate: NHTSA, as reported by the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO-11-603).

Exploration

Real complaints first, then pencil.

I pulled every NHTSA owner complaint for three cars that were later recalled, the 2019 Bolt EV, 2013 Fusion and 2012 Grand Cherokee, and grouped them by part and warning sign. Then I sketched the whole flow on paper.

Exploration · round one

Round one looked finished.

Type a VIN, pick a part on the car, read the case file, then choose Investigate, Escalate or Recall, with Recall already picked and an “irreversible” confirm. The driver got a banner in the car and a notification on the phone. Testing showed those last two screens were my biggest mistake.

Testing

The problems were bigger than layout.

Something felt off, so I went back to my old dealership and asked the people who live with recalls. I walked roughly 30 former coworkers through round one, reviewed every screen against Nielsen’s 10 heuristics, and sat down with Cliff, my former manager. His take: the longer a recall takes to find, the bigger the damage.

Solution · engineer

The engineer brings evidence. People make the call.

Every change answers a problem from testing. Pick a feature to see it, or start with the walkthrough.

Solution · driver

The driver never has to read while moving.

Designed in a real car, the way James Rampton taught. A recall is a safety moment, so the car never asks the driver to read while moving. Every alert has its own sound.

While driving

A red recall icon behind the wheel and an alert sound. Nothing to read at speed.

In Park

The full message, the words GM already uses with owners, and one switch: limit charge to 90%.

Until the repair

Safety mode keeps charging at 90% and reminds the driver each time they park.

Fixed

When the dealer records the repair, the car confirms it and charging goes back to 100%.

The colors are a system the driver learns once: red is the recall, amber is safety mode, green is fixed.

Engineering × design

Every decision holds up in code.

I prototyped the data side too, so every design decision had to hold up in code.

01No hindsight, by design.

The case file can only read what existed on the flag date.

case_file.sqlSQL
SELECT * FROM complaints
WHERE part = :part
AND received <= :flag_date
ORDER BY received;
02An alert that matches the stakes.

One day at a time, never peeking at future reports.

Reflection

What I’d carry forward.

01Design the hand-off, not the verdict.

The most important button was the one I removed. Keeping the recall decision with the people accountable for it made the tool more trustworthy, not less.

02Test against history, not just opinions.

Interviews told me the case file felt scrambled. Only the replay showed my first rule would have missed a real recall.

03Be honest about the limits.
  • Complaints are not proof. A pattern is worth a look, not a cause.
  • The report groupings are my reading. Another reader might count a few differently.
  • Pushing an alert to the car screen, and the in-car haptic, would need GM’s vehicle team to confirm.
